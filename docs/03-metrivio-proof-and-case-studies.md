# Metrivio Proof & Case Study Document

**Purpose:** Defines exactly what credibility claims are and are not permitted, for the freelance developer and for the Skill. This document draws a hard line the Skill must never cross: Metrivio's founder's professional experience is real and usable; Metrivio's client results do not yet exist and must never be implied.

---

## PART A — Current Proof (What Can Legitimately Be Claimed)

### A.1 Confirmed Professional Experience

The following is confirmed, founder-provided background and may be used **accurately and precisely**, always framed as the founder's professional experience — never as Metrivio client work:

- Managed approximately $10–15M in annual advertising investment across channels for **Unilever**
- Led digital strategy/analytics work for **P&G**
- Experience in **financial services performance marketing**

### A.2 Rules for Using This Experience

1. It must always be attributed to the founder personally (e.g., "Metrivio's founder has managed $10–15M in annual ad investment for Unilever"), never phrased in a way that implies Unilever or P&G were Metrivio clients or that Metrivio delivered these results.
2. It establishes credibility for the *type of analytical and strategic judgment* Metrivio applies — cross-channel budget allocation thinking at meaningful scale — not a specific outcome guarantee for any Metrivio client.
3. No dollar savings, ROAS figures, or performance outcomes tied to this work should be stated unless the founder confirms a specific, accurate figure. This document does not currently contain any such figure, so none should be invented or assumed.
4. Financial services experience should be described generally ("experience in financial services performance marketing") unless the founder provides more specific, confirmed detail.

### A.3 What This Experience Does NOT Establish

- It does not establish that Metrivio (the business) has delivered any specific result for any client.
- It does not establish DTC/ecommerce-specific case history — Unilever and P&G are large enterprise/CPG contexts, not DTC/ecommerce brands in the $1M–$10M range. This is a real gap and should be described honestly as adjacent, transferable expertise, not equivalent experience.

## PART B — No Case Studies Yet (How to Handle Early-Stage Honestly)

Metrivio is early-stage and has no client case studies. This should be presented as a fact of where the business is today, not as an apology or a weakness to explain away. The honest positioning rests on five things Metrivio *does* have:

1. **Independent expertise** — the founder's confirmed background in large-scale marketing budget allocation (Section A.1)
2. **A defined diagnostic methodology** — the STOP/FIX/SCALE/TEST/REALLOCATE framework, which is a structured, repeatable process, not an ad hoc opinion
3. **A sample diagnostic/report** — a concrete example of what the deliverable looks like and the caliber of thinking applied (this is the single strongest proof asset available pre-client, and should be positioned as such everywhere: website, sales calls, outbound)
4. **A clear, specific process** — defined steps, defined timeline, defined deliverables (reduces the "will this actually happen and will it be useful" risk a prospect feels)
5. **Transparency itself** — explicitly telling a prospect "Metrivio is a new, independent practice; here's exactly what you'll get and here's the reasoning behind the diagnostic" is a credibility move, not a liability, for the type of buyer being targeted (a founder who values independent judgment will respect directness over inflated claims)

**Language the Skill and outbound copy should use:** honest, specific, forward-looking. Something like "Metrivio is a newly independent diagnostic practice" paired immediately with the concrete methodology and sample report — never a claim of an established client base or track record with named results.

**Language to avoid entirely:** any implication of "trusted by," "clients include," "results for brands like," or similar language that implies an existing client roster, until real clients and permissions exist.

### Rules for Using This Template

1. Never fill in a field with a plausible-sounding placeholder "for now" — an empty, honestly labeled field is always preferable to an invented one.
2. Never present an ESTIMATED or CLIENT-REPORTED figure as VERIFIED.
3. Never publish without explicit, documented client permission.
4. As real case studies accumulate, they should progressively replace the Part B "no case studies yet" positioning across the website, sample report messaging, and outbound copy — this document's Part B framing is a temporary bridge, not a permanent identity.
